# RED PLANET CORPORATE

*"The failed expeditions of the past century have taught us that Mars requires both
corporate innovation and collaborative infrastructure. Success demands competition for
advancement, cooperation for survival."*
— Mars Colonial Charter, Preamble, 2159

A traditional NRP (Nation Roleplay): one host, PM-submitted turns, host discretion fills
every gap not covered below. There is no app, dashboard, or spreadsheet to learn — you send
the host a turn, the host tells you what happened.

---

## 1. The world, briefly

Earth's governments collapsed under population pressure in the late 21st century. Between
2087–2097, the surviving megacorporations consolidated into six entities that now govern
the Earth-Moon system under the **Corporate Accords of 2098**. The Moon became a shared
industrial zone; between 2140–2156 it proved that permanent off-world colonies work.

Mars has eaten three prior attempts — **Prometheus** (2112, agricultural systems failed),
**New Olympia** (2134, cascading infrastructure failure, 16 dead), **Ares Station** (2145,
an unmanned mission that went silent). Each failure is why this attempt looks the way it
does: managers instead of explorers, mandatory cooperation on survival-critical systems,
everything else left to corporate competition.

**The Mars Colonial Charter (2159)** is the actual premise of the game: *compete for
advancement, cooperate for survival.* Emergency Protocol Mars-Alpha makes that literal — no
corporate territory or equipment is off-limits during a declared colony-wide emergency, full
stop, disputes get resolved after the crisis. Outside of a declared emergency, you're
running your subdivision against five rivals with no such restriction.

Communication lag to Earth is 4–24 minutes each way. In practice, that means the host (and
therefore Mars Colonial Authority, in-fiction) has real autonomy, and so do you — this is a
game where the rules can't and won't cover everything, by design.

## 2. The six corporations

Pick one when you register your subdivision.

| Corporation | Domain |
|---|---|
| Terra Agricultural Syndicate | Food production, hydroponics, vertical farming, life-support biology |
| Unified Mining Consortium | Extraction, energy, resource processing |
| Stellar Dynamics Corporation | Transport, logistics, aerospace |
| Helix Pharmaceutical Group | Healthcare, biotech, genetic research |
| Omega Security Solutions | Defense, surveillance, security |
| Genesis Tech Industries | R&D, computing, communications |

Your corporation is flavor and a soft lean, not a hard class — a Genesis Tech subdivision
that wants to specialize in agriculture can, it'll just make more narrative sense to lean
into what your corporation is good at when you ask the host for something.

## 3. Getting started

Tell the host:

- **Subdivision name** and **parent corporation** (§2).
- **Manager's name** and a short pitch for who they are — corporate lifer, disgraced
  researcher pulled off the bench, whatever fits.
- Whatever starting focus you want (an economic lean, a security lean, a research lean).
  There is no fixed starting stat block — the host will tell you your subdivision's opening
  position based on your corporation and pitch.

You'll be assigned a starting **Headquarters** and enough personnel and resources to make
your first few turns' worth of moves. Ask the host for specifics once you're in.

---

## 4. Resources

Six resource types, tracked loosely by the host (exact numbers matter less than trend —
you're not required to reconcile a ledger every turn):

| Resource | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Credits | Universal currency |
| Energy | Powers everything |
| Minerals | Construction |
| Water | Life support + agriculture |
| Food/Bio | Feeds your people |
| Research | Unlocks new capabilities |

You build facilities and staff them with your people to generate resources. There's no
per-unit upkeep spreadsheet — if your subdivision is clearly overextended (too many
ambitious builds, not enough people or income to support them), the host will tell you, and
it'll show up as a problem in your next report rather than a silent number going negative
somewhere you can't see.

---

## 5. Turn cycle

- **1 turn = 1 week**, adjustable at host discretion based on how the game's actually
  running.
- **Submit privately to the host** (PM/DM), by whatever deadline the host sets for that
  cycle.
- **Late submission = hold position** — your subdivision does nothing new this turn, no
  resources spent, no actions taken. No exceptions, submit on time.
- **Results are posted** at the start of the next cycle: a public standings/events post for
  everyone, plus a private report to you covering anything you wouldn't want a rival to see.

---

## 6. Submitting a turn

Every turn, write up to:

- **5 Operations actions** — building, extracting, researching, expanding territory,
  training/assigning personnel, trading, surveying. This is the bulk of what you do.
- **1 Corporate Action** — your parent corporation stepping in: a specialized favor,
  emergency resource pull, or company-specific move (ask the host what's on the table for
  your corporation, or propose something in-genre and let them price it).
- **1 Diplomatic or Public Relations action** — negotiate, form or break an alliance,
  gather intelligence on a rival, make a public statement, denounce someone, run a PR push.
  Pick one lane per turn, not both.
- **1 secret action** — anything you want kept off the public record: espionage, a quiet
  approach to a rival, a hidden contingency. The host will not reveal this happened unless
  your target detects it.

You don't need a rigid template. A turn submission is just: what you're doing, in plain
language, organized roughly into those four buckets so the host can find everything. Say
what you want to happen if something you're attempting fails or gets contested (a
contingency instruction) — don't make the host guess.

**Sub-actions are fine.** If one of your 5 Operations actions naturally has a few small
parts (e.g., "build a Research Lab, and staff it with researchers pulled from my Habitat"),
that's one action, not three. If you're clearly trying to cram an entire turn's worth of
distinct maneuvers into a single declared action to dodge the cap, the host will tell you to
prioritize — and if you push it anyway, expect some of it to just fail.

---

## 7. Buildings & facilities

There's no fixed building catalog with pre-set costs. When you declare a build, describe
what you want (a Research Lab, a bigger Habitat, a Defense Installation, a Trade Depot,
whatever fits) and roughly how ambitious it is; the host assigns a resource cost and a
completion time (usually the following turn for something modest, longer for something
major) on the spot. Common facility types you'll probably want at some point:

- **Extraction Site / Power Facility / Water Reclamation / Bio-Dome** — the resource
  generators. You need these running to have anything else to spend.
- **Habitat** — houses personnel; you'll need more as your subdivision grows.
- **Research Lab** — where Research gets generated and spent.
- **Communications Array** — your intelligence and diplomacy hub.
- **Defense Installation** — makes your subdivision harder to raid, sabotage, or push
  around territorially.
- **Trade Depot** — where you sell resources and deal with other subdivisions directly.

Once built, a facility does what it says on the tin every turn it's staffed — you don't need
to re-declare "harvest resources" as an action every cycle unless something's changed;
ongoing production is handled passively by the host between turns.

---

## 8. Personnel

Your subdivision has people — roughly grouped by role (Engineers for construction and
extraction, Contractors for security, Administrators for economy and diplomacy, Researchers
for Research, Analysts for intelligence). You don't need to track exact headcounts turn to
turn; describe who you're assigning where when it matters ("send my Engineers to the new
Extraction Site"), and the host will flag it if you're clearly overcommitted or
under-defended somewhere.

---

## 9. Diplomacy, intelligence, and public relations

- **Alliances and agreements** carry social weight, not mechanical enforcement. If you
  and another subdivision agree to something, the host notes it publicly (or privately, if
  you both want it that way) — breaking it has no automatic penalty beyond the fact that
  everyone now knows you broke it.
- **Intelligence gathering** (surveillance, investigation, quiet approaches to a rival) is
  resolved by host judgment: describe what you're trying to find out or do, and the host
  weighs it against whatever the target has invested in security/secrecy. You may or may not
  get caught; if you do, you'll find out from the host, not from a probability table.
- **Public Relations** (statements, press releases, denouncements, PR campaigns) get posted
  to the public game channel more or less as you write them. A false accusation that gets
  disproven costs you socially, not mechanically — the colony remembers, the rulebook
  doesn't need to.

---

## 10. Conflict, sabotage, territory disputes

There's no formula. If you attempt something adversarial against another subdivision
(sabotage, contesting a territorial claim, intercepting a move, an outright raid), the host
weighs what each side has actually invested — personnel committed, defenses built,
narrative setup — and rules on the outcome. Big mismatches resolve predictably in favor of
the stronger side; close calls get a coin-flip. You'll be told the outcome, not the math
behind it.

**Life support and colony-critical infrastructure are off-limits, always** — sabotaging a
rival's specific facility is fair game, sabotaging the systems that keep people alive is not
a competitive tactic, it's a hard line.

---

## 11. Emergencies

When the host declares a colony-wide emergency, Emergency Protocol Mars-Alpha kicks in:
competitive restrictions are suspended, subdivisions are expected to help each other, and
refusing to assist when asked has real consequences. Once the emergency's resolved, ordinary
competition resumes — and yes, being visibly better-prepared for the crisis than your
rivals is a legitimate advantage to walk away with.

---

## 12. Standings

The host posts periodic standings — roughly along the lines of Economic, Industrial,
Research, Territorial, Security, and Intelligence strength — based on overall judgment of
how each subdivision is doing, not a recalculated formula every turn. Standings are a
narrative snapshot for bragging rights and situational awareness, not a hard leaderboard
driving mechanical bonuses.

---

## 13. Market & trade

Sell resources to Earth at whatever flat rate the host is currently offering (it can move
over time as the colony's economy develops — the host will announce changes). Trading
directly with another subdivision is a straightforward negotiation between the two of you;
report the agreed terms to the host so it's on record. There's no live price-elasticity
market and no subdivision stock/equity system — trade is between people, not against an
algorithm.

---

## 14. Conduct & house rules

- **Complaints and disputes go to the host privately.** Litigating a ruling in the public
  channel gets it ignored; ask in PM.
- **Send your turns on time.** The host is processing every subdivision's turn by hand —
  showing up late and expecting special treatment isn't reasonable.
- **Host discretion is final** on anything these rules don't cover. That's not a loophole,
  it's the actual rules engine — this document sets the frame, the host fills it in.
  Genuinely ambiguous edge cases get resolved in the spirit of fairness and internal
  consistency.
- **Don't be a jerk to the host.** This game runs on someone volunteering real time and
  effort every week. Pushing your luck when a ruling doesn't go your way, or spamming/
  trolling the game channel, is the fastest way to get sidelined.

---

## 15. What happened to the old version

Red Planet Corporate started with a much heavier design — an interactive dashboard, a
24-sheet mechanical rulebook, per-unit action budgets, live market formulas, a subdivision
stock market, a vehicle-building mini-game. That's all preserved in [`/archive`](./archive)
if anyone wants to look at it or revive pieces of it later, but the game is being run from
this document going forward. If something below turns out to be missing a rule the old
version had an answer for, that's fine — ask the host and it becomes a ruling, the same way
it would in any other NRP.
